What this is

Three traditional clocks, all counted from the local sunrise rather than midnight. Before today's sunrise, you are still in yesterday's day. The NOW tab shows where you are in each; TIMELINE lets you plan; LOG is for testing the swara prediction against your own breath.

Reading the sun bar

The thin bar under the title runs from sunrise to the next sunrise. The gold stretch is daylight, the dot is now.

1 · Planetary Hour

Sunrise→sunset is cut into 12 "day hours", sunset→sunrise into 12 "night hours". They are only 60 minutes long at the equinox. The first day hour belongs to the planet of the weekday (Sun on Sunday, Moon on Monday, Mars on Tuesday, Mercury on Wednesday, Jupiter on Thursday, Venus on Friday, Saturn on Saturday). From there the rulers follow the Chaldean order: Saturn ♄, Jupiter ♃, Mars ♂, Sun ☉, Venus ♀, Mercury ☿, Moon ☽, repeating. The card shows the ruling planet, which hour of day or night you are in, time left, and the next ruler.

2 · Tattva Tide

The Golden Dawn teaches that five elemental currents (tattvas) flow in turn, each for 24 minutes, starting with Akasha at sunrise:

    One full round is two hours and repeats all day and night. Each tattva also contains all five as sub-tattvas of 4.8 minutes. "Tejas of Apas" means the Tejas sub-current inside the Apas tide. The ring on the card is one two-hour round; the gold dot is now. The small symbol is the sub-tattva, the large one the main tattva.

    Sources disagree on two details, so both are settings: whether Prithvi or Apas comes last, and whether a tattva's sub-tattvas start with its own (Vayu of Vayu, Tejas of Vayu…) or always with Akasha. The "Rama Prasad unequal" duration mode is included but its numbers are not verified against the source; see the note in Settings.

    3 · Swara · Nadi

    Shiva Swarodaya says the breath favours one nostril at a time. Ida (left, lunar) or Pingala (right, solar) opens the day at sunrise depending on the lunar day (tithi): in the waxing fortnight (Shukla paksha) tithis 1–3 are Ida, 4–6 Pingala, 7–9 Ida, and so on; the waning fortnight (Krishna paksha) starts with Pingala. The side then switches every 60 minutes (2½ ghatikas). Around each switch there is a short Sushumna window where both flow; it is marked and its width is a setting.

    The card shows the predicted side (L, both, R), the tithi with its paksha and Sanskrit name, and the time to the next switch.

    The experiment (LOG)

    Close one nostril, breathe, swap. Whichever is clearer is "flowing". Tap LEFT, RIGHT or BOTH. Each tap stores the time, what your body showed and what every clock predicted. The stats compare your body with the swara prediction overall, by time of day and by tithi. "Side only" ignores Sushumna windows and compares left/right alone. Export as CSV any time.

    Timeline

    A strip from this sunrise to the next with three lanes: planetary hours, tattvas and nadi. Drag sideways to scroll, tap to read all values at that moment, use ‹ › to look at other days. Planetary hours are shaded, not coloured; the tattva colours are the only bright things on purpose.

    Offline & install

    Once loaded over https (or localhost) the app caches itself. On iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen. On Android: menu → Install app. Settings and the log live in this browser's storage only.

    Assumptions

    • Sunrise/sunset/moon phase come from the suncalc library (±1–2 min).
    • Tithi is derived from the Moon–Sun elongation angle (1/30 of a lunation each) and read at sunrise.
    • Weekday for the planetary hour is the local solar weekday of the sunrise, independent of your phone's time zone.
    • Everything here is for personal experiment, not a claim about anything.
